Grifols: project tracking and reporting

Project status at a manufacturing plant lived in separate area lists and a hand-built weekly slide. I built the tracker, scoring dashboard and requirements documents that gave operations leadership one view.

Role
Project Management Intern, Operational Excellence, Grifols Biologicals, June to September 2026
Worked with
Operations leadership, Quality, Validation, Engineering, Facilities and QA
Built in
Excel, Microsoft Copilot and Word, and document templates
Status
Delivered during the internship. The scoring dashboard was demonstrated with sample data; standing use is not claimed here.

Problem and user

A planned shutdown touches 8 production areas, many owners and several functions. Status sat in area-level lists, and a weekly slide pulled it together by hand. Operations leadership needed one place to see what was late or blocked, and project reviews needed a consistent way to compare projects.

Key decisions

  • Let formulas find the delays. Every activity carries one of four statuses, and formulas compare end dates with today. Late items flag themselves, so nobody has to remember to check.
  • Match the format people already read. The monthly calendar shows only ramp down and ramp up for each area, because the weekly slide used that format. Each area also has its own tab with a day-by-day calendar.
  • Make the scoring model runnable by someone else. Projects are scored on five weighted factors. Dropdowns control the inputs and a guidance tab explains the steps, so a user enters facts about a project and the sheet does the arithmetic. A project with no sponsor is flagged automatically.

What I built and coordinated

  • Ongoing-project tracker. A front page with status by area and building, a timeline of the early milestones that gate everything else (approvals and work permits), an activity list per area, a rollup, and Gantt views that move when a date changes.
  • Scoring dashboard for C-suite project reviews. KPI tiles, a RAG health summary and charts that let leaders compare projects side by side.
  • Requirements and design documentation. User requirements and system design templates, and a workbook that links each requirement to a design and a test, built with Engineering and QA.
  • Meeting-minutes workflow. A Microsoft Copilot workflow connecting Excel and Word in both directions (Excel to Word and Word to Excel) that replaces manual meeting-note preparation. It transcribes the meeting, identifies action items and the responsible person or team, and prepares a Word document. A person reviews the draft before it goes to participants. It gives meetings structured documentation, tracked actions and clear ownership.
  • Shutdown coordination. Milestone sign-offs and blocked activities for the 2026 summer shutdown, coordinated across Quality, Validation, Engineering and Facilities.

Evidence and results

  • Weekly reporting preparation went from roughly 1 day to 1 hour, including review, an estimated 87% reduction in working time. This is my recalled estimate, not a timed study.
  • I found updates easier and reporting more accurate. I have no error counts, so I give no accuracy figure.
  • I audited the tracker formula by formula. The pass found leftover rows in the rollup that a visual check had missed, and activities whose status no longer matched the calendar date. Both were fixed.
  • Blocked and late items showed up while there was still time to act on them.

Limitations and next step

  • I have no records of the meeting-minutes workflow's volume, time saved or accuracy, so the page gives none.
  • Company data, people and equipment names are left out. The images on this page use made-up numbers.
  • I demonstrated the scoring dashboard with sample data in my internship review and recommended it for quarterly portfolio review. Whether the team adopted it is not stated here.
