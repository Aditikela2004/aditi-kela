Husky Flex Wallet: from meal plan to marketplace

Students prepay for a meal plan and still spend cash elsewhere. Our team proposed a unified wallet that keeps spending inside the university's ecosystem.

Type
Team project: slide deck and supplementary analysis
Course
Northeastern University graduate course
Frameworks
TAM/SAM/SOM, JTBD, RICE, North Star metric, go-to-market, risk and trade-offs
My role
Project manager and lead researcher on a team of three to four
$120Mestimated yearly campus dining flow: 20,000 students × $6,000
65% to 92%wallet utilization, current to target
$12-15Mestimated first-year obtainable value

The problem

Students opt out of campus dining because of limited hours and variety, so prepaid value goes unused while they spend cash off campus. Freshmen must buy a $6,000 plan, and many report wasting thousands in unused swipes. The team called this revenue leakage.

The idea

Husky Flex Wallet: one wallet and one balance. It turns swipes, dining dollars and card credit into flexible value that works at campus dining and a small set of approved local vendors. Dining services shift from sole provider to marketplace platform.

The numbers

  • Total market: about 20,000 students × $6,000, or about $120 million a year. Serviceable: about 70%, or $84 million. First-year obtainable: 15 to 20% of spend through an upperclassmen pilot, or $12 to $15 million.
  • North Star: Wallet Utilization Rate. Using conservative case assumptions, students consume $3,900 of the $6,000, or 65%. The target is 92%, which recovers $1,620 per student without raising prices.
  • RICE ranking favored software over buildings: Merchant API integration scored 7.7, longer dining hours 2.1, and a new dining hall 0.4 (scores shown in thousands in our supplementary document).

Risks we made explicit

  • Revenue erosion, since the university earns when plans go unused. Response: start with an optional upperclassmen pilot and capture the cash students already spend elsewhere.
  • Merchant overload. Response: sign five partners in months one to three, test payments in months four to six, then scale.
  • Student trust. Response: an in-app view showing where funds go.

How I led the team

  • Split the work by section and gave each teammate an owner, a deadline and a checkpoint before the due date.
  • Tracked tasks and progress in Excel.
  • The hard part was getting everyone into the same meeting. We split the work instead, and each person finished their part on time by coordinating directly with the teammate whose section touched theirs, within agreed timeframes.
  • I checked in with everyone for updates and brought their ideas together into one conclusion.
  • Merged everyone's work into one consistent deliverable: a slide deck and a supplementary analysis that stay consistent with each other.
  • Led the final delivery. We delivered on time and received strong feedback. Our score went from 85% to 94%.

What I took from it

The competition here came from DoorDash, cash, and a student who quietly stops using what they paid for. Naming that behavioral competitor changed which features mattered.
