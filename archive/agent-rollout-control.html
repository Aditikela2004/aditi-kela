Agent rollout control center

A concept board that tracks every AI agent a company runs, scores each one's risk, and puts explicit checks and role-based sign-offs between pilot and live.

Why I built it

I coordinated stakeholder requests and prioritization on an IT HR team at RBL Bank, and worked in risk and compliance. The lesson I took was that people follow a process when the next step is obvious. This board applies that to AI agents. All agents are synthetic.

The problem

Agents are easy to start and hard to keep track of. Without one list, nobody knows how many exist, who owns them, what they cost or which can act on their own. Moving to production then depends on who happens to ask the right questions.

What I built

  • A fleet board of eleven agents with risk, cost against budget, days since review and a four-stop stage rail.
  • A risk tier built from autonomy, the data an agent can read and customer exposure, with the reasons shown.
  • Stage gates that grow as an agent moves toward live.
  • A sign-off flow where higher-risk agents need more roles to approve.
  • An overview with cost against budget, stage and risk mix, and a needs-attention list.

Decisions and tradeoffs

  • Show the reasons behind the risk tier. An owner can see what to change. The weakness: a simple sum treats the factors as equal.
  • Checks first, sign-offs second. A request with open checks shows as blocked and says what to fix, so approvers do not review something that cannot pass.
  • More sign-offs for higher risk, fewer for Pilot. Low-risk work keeps moving, and a pilot exists to learn. Pilots that touch real data still depend on the data review gate.
  • No skipped stages. Each stage adds evidence. The cost: urgent agents cannot be fast-tracked in this version.
  • A needs-attention list instead of a long report. Five rules (no owner, no budget, over budget, overdue review, high risk with no fallback) cover the first things to chase.

What I tested

  • Twenty-one unit tests, a production build and a headless-browser click-through, including approving a request and moving an agent to Live.

What I have not tested yet

  • No real users, so no claims about time saved or incidents avoided.
  • Scoring weights and gates have not been reviewed by security or legal staff. Sign-offs are buttons, not real identities.

What I would do next

  • Ask three to five platform or governance people to set up their own fleet and say which checks they would add or drop.
  • Pull cost and usage from a mock billing feed.
  • Add an audit trail of who approved what and when.
  • Pause a live agent automatically when its evaluation fails.
