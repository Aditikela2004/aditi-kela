Just-in-time Kanban inventory template

A supply team asked for inventory control based on pull and visual signals. I built a workbook where every item tells you whether to reorder now, soon or not at all.

Role
Built it for the supply team
Built in
Excel
Principles
Just-in-time and Kanban
Skills shown
Kanban, just-in-time inventory, supply chain, process improvement, Excel

The idea

Just-in-time means ordering when stock needs it, not when a calendar says so, and Kanban turns that into a signal anyone can read. The workbook applies both to materials in a regulated plant, where lot numbers, expiry dates and storage conditions matter.

How it is organized

A dashboard counts items by signal. The item master holds each item with its lead time, average daily use, safety stock, supplier and storage condition, and calculates the reorder point and the signal. A card view lets you pick an item and see its Kanban card. A replenishment log records each order triggered. A transactions tab keeps a running balance by lot and expiry, and a supplier master holds lead times and approval status.

The signal logic

The reorder point is lead time multiplied by average daily use, plus safety stock. Green means on-hand stock sits above it. Amber means stock has dropped to the reorder point, so an order should go out now. Red means stock has dropped to the safety level and the order is urgent. Because lead time and daily use drive the math, the same rule works for a slow powder and for a fast consumable.

A note on the sample

The template ships with fictional items and suppliers so the signals can be seen working. Real item data would be loaded by the team that owns it.
