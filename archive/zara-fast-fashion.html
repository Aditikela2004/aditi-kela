Zara: when the product promise outgrows the operating model

Zara's speed and novelty built one of retail's strongest operating models. New EU rules now charge for what that model ignored. I looked at what has to change in the system, not only the supply chain.

Frameworks

The thesis

Zara's current tension traces to a measurement and governance system that rewarded forward-flow commercial performance and never counted lifecycle costs.

What I found

  • The supply chain was built for forward flow. Returns and end-of-life garments were secondary, which is now a compliance liability.
  • Hard-to-reverse decisions are physical (the logistics hub, nearshore supplier relationships) and organizational (a governance model built for speed and quality, not traceability depth).
  • A garment that sells through at full price in two weeks scores perfectly on every current metric, even if it is non-recyclable and untraceable.

What I recommended

  • Compare operating models before and after across four areas: supply chain direction, supplier governance, product design and performance system.
  • Make EPR diversion rate and traceability coverage primary KPIs, in the same dashboards and reviews as sell-through.
  • Turn traceability into a gate for volume allocation in supplier scorecards.
  • Put carbon intensity and fee exposure into product margin calculations, so designers see the cost when they choose a material.
  • Judge regional leaders on compliance as well as sales.

Was it avoidable?

Partly, at two points: when the supplier network scaled beyond Spain and Portugal without traceability depth, and when product costing ignored end-of-life obligations. The paper ends with a product decision: what promise is Zara building toward, and is the model being redesigned for it, or reacting to each new rule?
