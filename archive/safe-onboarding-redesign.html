Safe-by-design onboarding redesign

A clickable sign-up flow for a made-up app where protections are set by age, locked settings explain themselves, and Block and Report are shown during setup. It comes with the rules behind it and a kit for testing it.

Why I built it

I chose a UI/UX project for this set because design decisions here carry real stakes: what a young person sees first decides what is protecting them. The app, Kitewing, is fictional, and the age rules are my own design choices, not legal advice.

The problem

Sign-up flows for young people tend to ask too little and leave everything open, or lock everything without saying why. Both leave people unsure what is protecting them.

What I built

  • A prototype with a path for each age group, including simulated parent steps for under 13 that send nothing and collect no contact details.
  • A username step with generated suggestions and plain-words hints.
  • A settings screen where each setting says who decides and locked settings give a reason.
  • A Block and Report demo at the end of setup.
  • A rules-by-age table generated from the same code as the prototype.
  • A usability test kit with a facilitator script, five tasks, a session recorder and CSV export. It starts empty.

Decisions and tradeoffs

  • Defaults come from age, not a quiz. One neutral question, month and year. If the date is unclear it uses the younger group, and after an under-13 result the year cannot be retyped.
  • Who decides is visible. Every setting says Set for your age, Parent decides or Your choice.
  • Locked settings explain themselves. No bare Not available.
  • Opening up is allowed where appropriate, with a plain warning instead of a block.
  • Block and Report are shown while the person is calm, at the end of setup.

What I tested

  • Twenty-eight unit tests: age boundaries, parent flow, rule consistency across groups, username rules and the test-kit summary.
  • A production build and a headless-browser click-through of the under-13 and teen paths, all three tabs, dark mode and a phone-width view.

What I have not tested yet

  • No usability sessions yet. The test kit is built, and its results table stays empty until real sessions are entered.
  • No legal review of the rules. Stricter defaults may frustrate older teens, and the test kit is designed to look for that.

What I would do next

  • Run five or so sessions with adults, or with guardian consent, and revise the weakest task.
  • Review the rules with a policy or legal specialist.
  • Test the parent steps with real parents.
